Research notes

Research interests

NLP, LLMs and retrieval

Language systems that retrieve evidence, reason over it, and work alongside people.

  • Retrieval-augmented generation. Starting point: RAGTOP, where I compared index types for retrieval evaluation.
  • Multilingual NLP and factuality. Starting points: Probaho and Olikbochon, for English, Bangla and Banglish.
  • Efficient routing between model sizes. Starting point: SC-GRC, listed below.
  • AI agents. Systems that gather evidence and act on it with people in the loop.

Human-computer interaction

How people inspect evidence, calibrate trust, and stay in control of AI systems.

  • Human-centered evaluation. Asking whether recommendations improve user experience, not only system performance. Starting point: my early recommender-system work, listed below.

Algorithms and theory

Algorithms and theoretical computer science for search and reasoning.

SC-GRC: routing between small and large fine-tuned models

A follow-up to the thesis, unpublished. I fine-tuned Qwen2.5-3B and 7B with QLoRA on about 10,900 English, Bangla and Banglish question-answer pairs. A router decides, query by query, whether the small model's answer is good enough or the larger model should answer, using self-consistency, retrieval signals and a semantic cache. The benchmark has 333 queries (111 questions in three languages, across 37 categories) and compares five systems. I am re-validating the experiments before reporting results.

Human-Centric Recommendation Systems for Research Discovery

I'm exploring how recommendation models can support discovery on Researchar, a live platform for researchers. The planned evaluation compares a candidate recommender with baselines using in-platform feedback (satisfaction, trust and perceived relevance) and behavioral signals such as click history.
